# 02 — Unit economics, success rates, saturation, tariffs

> Researched 2026-11-03 by a web-search agent and edited during synthesis. **Evidence quality:** almost every margin, failure-rate and return number comes from vendor blogs that sell tools, courses or fulfillment. No independent study of dropshipping margins or failure rates was found. The most reliable inputs are the **eBay fee page**, the **NRF returns report** and the **Federal Register tariff notices**.

## 1. Worked example: a $30 eBay sale (AliExpress-sourced)

eBay fees (from the eBay fee page; the page is undated):

- **Final value fee: 13.6%** of the total sale (item, shipping and sales tax), up to $7,500.
- **Per-order fee:** $0.30 for orders of $10 or less, **$0.40** for orders over $10.
- eBay's managed payments are included in the final value fee, so **there is no separate PayPal/Stripe-style fee**. The agent's draft added 2.9% + $0.30 on top; that double count is removed here.

| Line | $ |
|---|---|
| Sale price, free shipping (buyer also pays ~7% sales tax, which eBay remits) | 30.00 |
| Product plus China shipping (assumed) | −15.00 |
| Final value fee: 13.6% × $32.10 (incl. tax) + $0.40 | −4.77 |
| Promoted Listings at 5% ad rate (assumed) | −1.50 |
| **Contribution before returns** | **8.73 (29%)** |
| Returns: 10% of orders × ($15 product lost + $10 return label) (assumed) | −2.50 |
| **Net per order** | **~6.23 (~21%)** |
| …if ~$3 import duty applies after de minimis ended (see §5) | **~3.20 (~11%)** |
| …and one INR/INAD case in 20 orders forfeits a full sale | **~1.70 (~6%)** |

**Takeaway:** the generic AliExpress→eBay model leaves single-digit to low-teens net margin. One bad week of returns or a supplier price rise can wipe it out. **Net margin needs a cost-of-goods ratio well under 50% of the sale price.** Most saturated products don't have that.

## 2. Returns, refunds, item-not-received

- **NRF 2025 (primary source):** online returns are **19.3%** of online sales, $849.9B in total. Vendor figures put apparel at 25–40% and electronics at 8–15%.
- **Shipping times:**
  - China-shipped: 2–5 weeks (some vendors say 30–60 days)
  - US warehouse: 4–10 days
  - Long delivery windows drive "item not received" disputes.
- **Chargebacks:** keep them under 1%. A rate around 8% is cited as an account-freeze risk (vendor sources).

## 3. Success and failure rates

- The widely repeated "80–90% fail in year one" comes from unnamed industry reports and course sellers. **No database of dropshipping outcomes exists.**
- The defensible statement is that most attempts don't become sustained businesses. The usual causes are thin margins, customer-acquisition cost and lack of differentiation.
- One vendor's "Sarah Chen" success story and its "90% fail" figure have no verifiable source.

## 4. Saturation and Temu/Shein

- Catalogs are identical because everyone sources from the same AliExpress and Alibaba suppliers, so **price becomes the only lever**.
- One vendor claims average AliExpress margins fell from 35% (2020) to 18% (2025). Unverified.
- **Temu, Shein and AliExpress Choice now sell the same items direct to consumers**, often more cheaply than a dropshipper can buy them. A buyer who searches the item on Temu finds the source.
- Temu's US weekly shoppers fell 19% from April 2024 to February 2025 (EMARKETER) after tariff-driven price increases. That slightly helps sellers with domestic stock.

## 5. Tariffs and de minimis (most reliable section)

- **US:**
  - Duty-free de minimis ($800) **ended for China/HK on 2025-05-02 and for all countries on 2025-08-29** (Federal Register, EO 14324).
  - Postal shipments first paid a specific per-item duty ($80/$160/$200 by country tier). From **2026-02-28** that changed to an ad valorem rate.
  - Suspension continued in April 2026. Non-postal modes were suspended **indefinitely** in June 2026.
  - **Check CBP for the current postal rate.**
- **EU:**
  - **€3 flat duty** on parcels under €150 since **2026-07-01**, an interim measure through 2028.
  - A handling fee of about €2–3 is expected later.
  - Sources disagree on whether the €3 applies per item or per line.
- **Effect:** the China-direct cost advantage that made AliExpress dropshipping work has been structurally reduced. **US/EU-warehoused suppliers are now relatively more competitive.**

## 6. Where margin may still exist (hypotheses, mostly vendor-sourced)

- **Niche hobby or replacement parts:** low competition, buyers search by part number.
- **Bundles and kits:** the buyer can't find the exact bundle on Temu.
- **Bulky or heavy items from domestic warehouses:** Chinese-direct shipping is uneconomic, and there's no per-parcel duty. This is reasoning only, with no source.
- **Products where presentation matters:** better images, compatibility charts, sizing guides. Here the "better listing" idea actually adds value.

## 7. What profitable sellers reportedly do differently

- They narrow to one niche.
- They move to **US/EU warehouse** stock.
- They **private-label** once a product proves itself.
- They build an owned audience (email, social), so repeat customers come back.

Every source for this is a vendor with something to sell. Treat it as a pattern, not data.

## TikTok Shop note (vendor-sourced)

- 6% referral fee, reported rising to 8% for non-food categories from 2026-08-04.
- Affiliate commissions of 8–20%.
- **New accounts since 2026-02-09 must use TikTok Shop Logistics.** That limits classic dropshipping.
